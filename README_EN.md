#### Hemp-Bio-DropStitch: Biodegradable Hemp Pneumatic-Frame Composite (Specification v1.2)

**Project Status:** Open Engineering Standard — Public Domain (CC0 1.0).

The technology is open for free copying, manufacturing, modification, and commercial use without patent restrictions or royalty fees.

#### Core Engineering Concept

**Hemp-Bio-DropStitch** is a roll-fed composite material with drop-stitch yarns for rapidly deployable inflatable structural panels (3×3×3 m modules, floor/roof slabs, floating pontoons, and flood arks) featuring **mechano-biological disposal** at the end of their service life.

The structure operates on the principle of a **composite pneumatic shell with a load-bearing dry core**:

- **Low operating air pressure — 1.5–3.0 PSI (0.10–0.20 atm):** Compressed air is used solely for initial deployment of the panel geometry and pre-tensioning of the envelope, eliminating excessive stress on the drop-stitch threads and enabling the use of natural fibers.
- **Load-bearing core:** Primary compressive mechanical loads and thermal insulation are borne by a dry compacted filler (calibrated hemp hurd + hydrated lime) blown inside after the panel is inflated.

#### 1. Manufacturing Process Specification

- **Reinforcing base (cord):** Dense plain- or twill-weave hemp fabric. Prior to lamination, the fabric undergoes **mandatory IR or convective drying to a residual moisture content of < 2%** to prevent steam formation and delamination.
- **Drop-stitch threads:** Density of **20–24 threads/cm²** (~2.2 mm thread pitch). High-twist spun hemp yarn with pre-stretching and bio-waxing is used.
- **Fiber preparation and bio-protection:** Water-soluble starch bio-sizing, enzymatic water washing, and natural tannin impregnation to prevent rotting.
- **Gas-impermeable barrier membrane:** Double-sided extrusion lamination with a **PHBH/PBAT** composite to a total thickness of **0.35–0.50 mm**.
  - *Component biodegradation status:* PHBH powder is certified under **EN 13432** for thicknesses up to 3.46 mm; PBAT pellets are certified for thin films, therefore the finished multilayer laminate (0.35–0.50 mm thick) is subject to confirmatory batch testing after shredding.
- **UV stabilization:** Addition of a mineral UV barrier (natural ochre, titanium dioxide, or carbon black) to the outer membrane layer + a removable protective cover made of impregnated hemp canvas for multi-year outdoor exposure.
- **Perimeter welding:** Adaptation of standard hot-wedge or high-frequency (HF) welding lines (operating temperature **165–195 °C**).

#### 2. Thermal Physics and Filler Blow-In Protocol

- **Dry blow-in composition:** Calibrated hemp hurd (**1–4 mm** fraction) + **5% dry hydrated lime Ca(OH)₂** without adding water.
- **Filling technology:** Pneumatic blow-in through multi-point distribution ports (manifold header) with simultaneous vibration compaction of the panel (vibration parameters are specified in the OEM manufacturer's process sheet) to eliminate voids.
- **Working bulk density:** **110–140 kg/m³**.
- **Thermal conductivity (λ):** **0.055–0.065 W/(m·K)** (baseline laboratory value for dry loose hemp hurd; the 5% lime additive is accounted for as a factor slightly increasing λ relative to pure hurd and is verified during batch testing).

#### Thermal Resistance (R-Value) and Panel Thickness Selection:

- **100 mm panel (baseline standard for Moscow and central regions):** Accounting for surface heat transfer resistance (R_si + R_se ≈ 0.16), it provides **R ≈ 1.70–1.98 m²·K/W**, meeting the normalized requirement under SP 50.13330 for Moscow with the regional coefficient m_p = 0.63 (≈ 1.88 m²·K/W), subject to specific energy consumption compliance. Due to the complete absence of thermal bridges in the monolithic envelope, total heat loss of a 3×3×3 m cube at −25 °C is only 1.2–1.4 kW.
- **200 mm panel (Urals, Siberia, autonomous "passive house"):** **R ≈ 3.2–3.8 m²·K/W** (fully exceeds the baseline Moscow standard of ≈ 3.0 m²·K/W without reduction coefficients).
- **300–400 mm panel (Arctic, Yakutia, extreme frosts down to −60 °C):** **R ≈ 4.8–7.5 m²·K/W**.

#### 3. Operational Properties, Repair, and Disposal

1. **All-weather performance and buoyancy:** Operating temperature range from **−60 °C to +65 °C**. Full perimeter airtightness ensures absolute buoyancy of modules during spring floods and inundations (with the option to flip the symmetrical cube "ceiling-down" on the water for drying and hull inspection).
2. **Modularity and maintainability:** Panels are joined via universal lacing (90°/180°). Minor mechanical punctures are sealed in 5 minutes using a PHBH/PBAT film patch and a hot-air gun; in case of critical damage, any panel can be unlaced and replaced individually.
3. **Microplastic-free disposal:** Under dry and intact operating conditions, the shell does not degrade. At the end of its service life, the panel is dismantled, shredded, and sent for industrial composting (**EN 13432**) or soil disposal, where it completely decomposes within **18–36 months** into water, CO₂, and humus.

#### 4. Order Parameters for OEM Manufacturers

- **R&D sample (pilot batch):** **100–200 m²** roll or prototype **3000×3000×100 mm** panels with a 100 mm lacing flap and manifold blow-in ports.
- **Industrial batch:** **3,000–5,000 m²** (kit for 50–80 autonomous 3×3×3 m modules).
